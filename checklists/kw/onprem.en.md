## Profile legend

- **ai**: AI and machine learning. Uses AI or ML models, including language models and agents
- **api**: APIs. Exposes or consumes application programming interfaces
- **contactless**: Contactless payments. Uses NFC, QR or other contactless payment technology
- **cots**: Acquired software. Commercial off the shelf or outsourced development
- **critical**: Business critical. Supports a critical business service with defined recovery objectives
- **digital**: Customer digital channel. Internet facing web or mobile channel used by customers
- **dlt**: Blockchain or DLT. Uses blockchain, distributed ledgers or smart contracts
- **email**: Sends email. Sends email or messages to customers or staff
- **fiveg**: 5G connectivity. Relies on 5G networks or edge computing
- **iot**: IoT or OT devices. Connects IoT or operational technology devices
- **newtech**: Other new technology. Adopts any other technology new to the entity
- **onboarding**: Digital onboarding. Enrols customers remotely and verifies their identity
- **openbanking**: Open banking. Exposes or consumes open banking APIs with third party providers
- **pay**: Payments. Processes payment transactions, cards or fund transfers
- **pii**: Personal data. Stores or processes personal data of customers or staff
- **remote**: Remote access. Reached remotely by staff, contractors or vendors
- **ssm**: Self-service machines. Serves ATMs, ITMs, kiosks or POS terminals
- **wallet**: Digital wallet. Provides or integrates a digital wallet

## What the levels mean

- **app**: Application. Evidenced by this application or engagement itself. These controls make up the score.
- **hosting**: Hosting. Evidenced by the environment that hosts it: data centre, network, endpoints. Counted in the score.
- **entity**: Entity. Owned by the entity wide programme such as the board, policies, audit and workforce. Shown as inherited and left out of the score unless you include it.

## Architecture extensions

This project's own checks. They are not regulator controls and they are scored separately.

### Post quantum readiness and crypto agility

- [ ] **ARCH-CRYPTO-1** (base): A cryptographic inventory of the application exists and is current: algorithms, key lengths, protocols, certificates, libraries and where each is used, in CBOM form. Relates to 5.7.1.3, 7.1.4.2.
- [ ] **ARCH-CRYPTO-2** (base): Every use of a quantum vulnerable algorithm that protects long lived confidential data has a named migration date, because such data can be harvested now and decrypted later. Relates to 7.1.4.1, 7.1.4.3.
- [ ] **ARCH-CRYPTO-3** (base): The design is crypto agile: algorithms, key sizes and libraries are configuration, and a swap does not require redesign or a data migration. Relates to 7.1.4.1.
- [ ] **ARCH-CRYPTO-4** (base): Transport endpoints support hybrid or post quantum key exchange where the platform allows it, and the remaining endpoints carry a dated plan. Relates to 7.1.4.1, 5.8.3.6.
- [ ] **ARCH-CRYPTO-5** (base): The application has its own milestone in the entity quantum readiness roadmap, with the systems and data at risk listed. Relates to 7.1.4.3.
- [ ] **ARCH-CRYPTO-6** (base): Post quantum algorithms were tested in a test environment for compatibility and performance and the results are recorded before production use. Relates to 7.1.4.4.
- [ ] **ARCH-CRYPTO-7** (base): Every vendor and third party component provides a post quantum roadmap, and contract examples such as RSA 2048 are treated as interim minimums. Relates to 7.1.4.3, 9.1.1.10.
- [ ] **ARCH-CRYPTO-8** (base): Long lived signatures such as code signing, document signing and audit trail sealing have a post quantum plan, since they must stay verifiable for years. Relates to 7.1.4.1, 5.8.1.11.

### Design artefacts

- [ ] **ARCH-DESIGN-1** (base): A threat model of the application is documented, covers every external interface and trust boundary, and is refreshed on major change. Relates to 5.8.1.4, 7.1.1.3.
- [ ] **ARCH-DESIGN-2** (base): A data flow diagram shows where classified data is stored, processed and transmitted, and which controls protect each hop. Relates to 5.3.3.2.
- [ ] **ARCH-DESIGN-3** (base): No secret lives in source code, images or configuration files: credentials, keys and tokens come from a managed vault and rotate on schedule. Relates to 5.6.1.9, 5.7.1.2.
- [ ] **ARCH-DESIGN-4** (base): Each component runs with the least privilege it needs, and service to service calls authenticate with workload identities rather than shared passwords. Relates to 5.1.3.1.

### AI application safety

- [ ] **ARCH-AI-1** (ai): Prompt injection and untrusted content are treated as attacks: model inputs from users, documents and tools are isolated from instructions and outputs are validated before use. Relates to 7.1.2.2.
- [ ] **ARCH-AI-2** (ai): Model provenance is known: weights, training data sources and third party model services are inventoried with their versions and licences. Relates to 7.1.2.1, 6.2.1.1.
- [ ] **ARCH-AI-3** (ai): Any agent or tool the model can call is scoped to the minimum permissions, and actions with money or data impact require a human decision. Relates to 7.1.2.4.
- [ ] **ARCH-AI-4** (ai pii): Prompts, retrieved context and outputs are logged for audit with personal data minimised, and customer data never trains an external model without a lawful basis. Relates to 7.1.2.3, 5.11.2.3.
- [ ] **ARCH-AI-5** (ai): The model has a rollback path and a kill switch, and drift or abuse alerts reach the incident process. Relates to 7.1.2.5.
